Why Does LoRA SFT Produce More Median Tokens (56.4k) Than Full SFT (39.7k) and Baseline (25.0k)?
There are three concrete reasons for this difference:
  1. Bimodal Token Usage (Fast Solves vs. Unsolved Timeouts): When SFT models solve and verify a task on the training demo pairs (~39% of attempts), they finish very fast—in a median of just 19,924 tokens on LoRA and 17,023 tokens on Full SFT, which is 30%–40% shorter than the Baseline's solved median (28,649 tokens). However, when SFT models cannot find a working rule (~61% of attempts), they refuse to submit an unverified guess and keep testing Python hypotheses until they hit the harness's hard token ceiling. Because the 50th-percentile (overall median) falls inside the ~61% unsolved bucket, the overall median simply reflects the hard token cap of the evaluation harness.
  2. Different Eval Token Caps (60k on LoRA vs. 40k on Full SFT vs. 48k on Baseline): LoRA SFT was evaluated with the raw E031 60,000-token cap (40 max turns, 6k thinking/turn), so unsolved attempts cluster at 56k–60k tokens (pulling the overall median to 56.4k). Full SFT was evaluated with our 40,000-token fast cap (32 max turns, 5k thinking/turn), clamping unsolved attempts at ~40k tokens (overall median 39.7k). Meanwhile, the untuned Baseline (h1, 20 turns, 3.5k thinking/turn) frequently gives up early and submits an unverified guess around 25.0k tokens.
  3. Low-Rank Adapter vs. Full-Weight Adaptation on Solved Tasks: Even among solved tasks, LoRA uses slightly more tokens than Full SFT (19.9k in 9 turns vs. 17.0k in 7 turns, with 92% vs. 88% <think> tokens). Updating only 1.59% of weights via LoRA imitates the surface verbosity of DeepSeek-R1's reasoning chains, whereas Full SFT (100% of weights, lower validation loss 0.7549 vs. 0.7627) directly adapts the 48 Gated DeltaNet recurrent states and MLP layers to reach valid Python rules in 2 fewer turns.

4 Conclusions & Next Steps

Key Takeaways & Recommended Kaggle Setup

Conclusions

  • SFT more than doubles N=2 accuracy (+27.8%): At N=2 attempts, LoRA SFT reaches 46.94% and Full SFT Step 100 reaches 40.76%, compared to 19.17% on the comparable N=2 baseline (h1).
  • SFT solves puzzles 30%–40% faster when it succeeds: Solved attempts take a median of only 17.0k–19.9k tokens (7–9 turns) on SFT models vs. 28.6k tokens on pure Qwen.
  • Full SFT reaches better validation loss than LoRA: Full SFT (0.7549) beats LoRA (0.7627), and its final checkpoint (Step 192) is already exported to FP8 awaiting evaluation.
  • SFT makes high accuracy fit inside the 6h Kaggle limit: Pure Qwen needs N=4 and 27.6M tokens (~12.2h) to reach ~47–49%, whereas SFT reaches 41–47% at N=2 in 10.4M–14.6M tokens (~4.6h–6.4h).

Future Directions (Immediate Next Steps)

  • 1. Evaluate Full SFT Step 192 Final: LoRA jumped +6.7% between Step 150 and Step 191. Full SFT has only been evaluated up to Step 100 (40.76%); evaluating Step 192 should capture that same late-epoch gain.
  • 2. Cap SFT attempts at 42k tokens + Gate Attempt 2: Since solved SFT runs finish in ~18k tokens, capping attempts at 42k tokens (and skipping/seeding Attempt 2 if Attempt 1 already passes demo pairs) keeps LoRA/Full SFT at ~10.4M tokens (~4.6 hours on 4×L4).
  • 3. Port h7's Two-Stage Think Nudge to the SFT Harness: Adding the graceful </think> nudge from h7 prevents SFT runs from timing out mid-thought before calling submit_answer.

5 Training Procedure & Systems Pipeline (Why Full SFT Took Only 75.5 Min)

Custom PyTorch FSDP2 + Triton Gated DeltaNet Kernels (No Megatron-LM Overhead)
What Training Framework Did We Use — and Why Was 27B Full SFT at 32k Context So Fast (75.5 Minutes)?
We did not use Megatron-LM or DeepSpeed. Instead, we wrote a custom, lightweight Native PyTorch FSDP2 (torch.distributed.fsdp.fully_shard) + Custom Triton Kernel trainer (train_full_sft.py + fast_deltanet.py) running on 8× NVIDIA B200 GPUs (1.43 TB HBM3e, NVLink 5). Full SFT (75.5 min total, 23.6s/step) ran 2.2× faster than 1-GPU LoRA SFT (2.7h, 51s/step) due to four engineering optimizations:
  1. True 8-Way Data Parallelism with Zero Gradient Accumulation (grad_accum = 1): 1-GPU LoRA had to run 8 sequential forward/backward passes per step (grad_accum=8). Full SFT processed 1 trajectory per B200 across 8 GPUs in a single parallel pass (grad_accum=1), overlapping FSDP2 all-gather and reduce-scatter over 1.8 TB/s NVLink 5.
  2. Custom Fused Triton Kernels for Qwen3.8's 48 Gated DeltaNet Layers (fast_deltanet.py): 48 of Qwen3.8-27B's 64 layers are Gated DeltaNet linear-attention layers. Standard HuggingFace falls back to a sequential 512-chunk Python loop at 32k context and materializes ~32 GB of intermediate recurrent states. Our custom @triton.jit forward/backward chunk-recurrent kernels keep the [128, 16] state in B200 SRAM and use a vectorized cuBLAS triangular solve, accelerating the 48 DeltaNet layers by ~4× and cutting state memory by 64×.
  3. Chunked Logits Cross-Entropy (logits_chunk_size = 2048) + FlashAttention-2: Materializing the full [1, 32768, 248320] FP32 logits tensor for Qwen's 248k vocabulary would allocate 32.5 GB per GPU. Computing lm_head + cross_entropy in 2,048-token chunks keeps peak logits memory under 2.0 GB.
  4. Vision Tower Stripping & Fused DTensor AdamW (foreach=True): Stripping the unused multimodal vision encoder before FSDP2 wrapping leaves 26.90B pure text parameters (3.36B FP32 master params/GPU ≈ 20 GB/GPU with AdamW states), peaking at 105.5 GB / 178.4 GB VRAM per B200.

Data & Filtering (E031 DeepSeek-R1 Rollouts)

  • Source: 2,500 multi-turn Python REPL trajectories from DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017 on arc2_train (1,070) and nvarc_b0 (1,430).
  • 3-Gate Verification Filter: Kept only trajectories that (1) passed 100% of training demo pairs in Python (train_pass=True), (2) explicitly called submit_answer, and (3) matched the exact ground-truth test grid (correct=True) → 1,907 verified rollouts (eliminating the 19.7% of NVARC demo-passing rollouts that overfit and fail on test).
  • Eval De-Contamination: Checked all 120 ARC-AGI-2 eval tasks under exact + $D_8$ dihedral rotation/reflection + color-permutation canonical hashes (blocking derivative parents 2753e76c & 81c0276b).
  • Parent-Grouped 90/10 Split & 32k Cap (Zero Truncation): Grouped by parent puzzle ID (0 parent overlap between train and val) and kept whole trajectories ≤ 32,768 tokens so no final submit_answer is ever cut off → 1,535 train trajectories (mean 18.7k tokens, 13.7 turns) and 64 val trajectories.

What We Train On (Assistant-Only Supervision)

  • Supervised Tokens (loss_mask = 1): Loss is computed strictly on the assistant's <think>...</think> reasoning blocks and <tool_call>...</tool_call> Python scripts.
  • Masked Out (loss_mask = 0): System instructions, tool definitions, initial puzzle grids, and all intermediate <tool_response> IPython outputs / budget notes are masked out.
  • Exact Global Token Normalization: Across all 8 micro-batches/GPUs, gradients are normalized by the exact global count of supervised assistant tokens in the step via dist.all_reduce(SUM).
  • Collaborative FP8 Export: Every 50 steps (and at the final step), all 8 FSDP2 ranks consolidate weights in CPU RAM and quantize all 8 GPUs in parallel to 128×128 block-wise FP8 (float8_e4m3fn) in ~25 seconds.

6 Comparison with Havu's Results from GitHub (hav4ik/arcprize2026-arcagi2)

Benchmarking Against Havu's E028 SFT, E032 SFT, E020 Pure Qwen & E027/E040 4×L4 Runs
Headline Comparison vs. Havu's GitHub:
Havu trained two Qwen3.8-27B LoRA SFT models via the external Tinker API using LoRA r=128 + Dimuon optimizer: E028 (on 64k-truncated E025 traces) and E032 (on 1,900 correct E031 traces ≤64k). On single-attempt RTX evaluation (60k cap), Havu's E032 scored 42.9% Task Pass@1 (40.1% Pair Pass@1)—closely matching our 1-attempt LoRA SFT Step 191 (39.17% Task Pass@1) and Full SFT Step 100 (36.55% at 40k cap). However, on Havu's 4×L4 Kaggle-pace runs (E027/E040), both E028 (24.7%) and E032 (12.5% on 48-pair probe, 37/48 out of budget) collapsed below his untuned Base model because 60k-cap attempts timed out on L4 GPUs. Our pipeline solves this exact bottleneck in three ways:
  1. Full 120-Task N=2 Kaggle Pass@2 Scaling (46.94% LoRA / 40.76% Full SFT): Havu only ran 1 attempt on the full eval set (and a 20-pair partial N=2 check). Running full N=2 train-verified selection across all 120 tasks lifts our LoRA SFT by +7.77 pp to 46.94% Official Kaggle Pass@2.
  2. Fitting the Student into the <6h 4×L4 Budget (10.35M Tokens): In E032, Havu noted: "37/48 out of budget... Next: fit the student to a ~29k share." By training strictly on ≤32k trajectories and evaluating Full SFT Step 100 under a 40k token cap, our solved median drops to 17.0k tokens (vs. Havu's 21.4k mean) and completes all 120 tasks (N=2) in 10.35M tokens (~4.6h on 4×L4) while scoring 40.76% Pass@2.
  3. 2× Larger Held-Out NLL Reduction via B200 AdamW / Full FSDP2: Havu's Tinker LoRA r=128 + Dimuon reduced held-out NLL by -0.058 nats (0.958 → 0.900). Our B200 AdamW LoRA r=64 reduced held-out NLL by -0.109 nats (0.8721 → 0.7627) and Full FSDP2 SFT by -0.117 nats (0.8721 → 0.7549) on a strict parent-grouped 90/10 split.
