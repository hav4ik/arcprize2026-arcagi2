B32-M OOM Modes

Why the B32-M submission (Manh's vLLM fork, DCP4) most likely scored 46.5 instead of ~70, and what to do differently.

Most likely cause: a GPU out-of-memory a few hours into the scored run. In Manh's fork, one CUDA OOM kills the whole engine. B32-M's solver then spent its retries on the dying server, so most of the remaining work was lost. Host RAM is ruled out.

Reproduced end to end on 2026-10-30: the pod replica with Kaggle-matched GPU headroom hit four CUDA OOMs (2.1, 4.0, 5.9 and 7.2 h), ran out of restarts and ended at 7.26 h with a final score of 50.4. Leaderboard 46.5; the same notebook with pod headroom scored 77.9. The pods run faster than Kaggle, so the same point comes later on Kaggle's clock. Not excluded as a second failure mode: a CUDA illegal memory access, which Manh hit on Kaggle at 120 concurrent requests.

The margin, hour by hour

How the margin disappears

  1. The KV pool is sized from a startup profile that misses long-context steps. DeepSeek-V4.1's sparse-attention indexer and decode logits grow with context length. B32-M's wheel (5e1bccd07c) predates the fork's profiling fixes (f506afa210, 7ab756940a), so it sized the pool as if those steps were free.
  2. Kaggle's startup leaves less room than the pods. CUDA graphs took 0.65 GiB on Kaggle vs 0.34 on pods. Add util 0.945 and DCP4, and the cap sat 0.67–0.73 GiB above reserved memory (pods: 1.16–1.22). That's 0.06–0.12 GiB below the profile's own peak.
  3. After warm-up the fork caps PyTorch at reserved + free − 0.5 GiB, so a stray allocation outside PyTorch can't fill the GPU. Every later working buffer has to fit under that fixed line.
  4. Peaks grow as contexts lengthen. On the replica, the first 10 minutes ate 0.4 GiB. The first allocator retry (a cache flush) came at 1.26 h, and a retry storm at 1.7 h (9 → 23 retries in 10 minutes). By 2 h the peak was growing faster (+0.09 GiB/h, up from +0.07), and at 2.11 h the replica hit a CUDA OOM.
  5. It runs out before zero. Memory gets stranded in partly used segments. On the replica a 32 MiB buffer failed with 20.06 GiB allocated under a 20.37 GiB cap, so ~0.3 GiB was stranded; the last sample before showed 0.40 GiB of spare room. Our local lab saw the same: an OOM with 0.39 GiB nominally free.
  6. One OOM ends the engine. vLLM has no per-request OOM recovery. Every in-flight attempt loses its progress, and B32-M's solver counted the dying server's HTTP 500s as failed tries.

What we do from now on

  • Pin the KV pool with --kv-cache-memory-bytes, sized from Kaggle's own startup numbers, so pods and Kaggle get the same margin. Util alone shifts with CUDA graphs and compile caches.
  • Keep spare room under the cap above stranded memory + 0.3 GiB on the worst GPU after warm-up, ideally ≥ 0.75 GiB. For B32-M's settings, util 0.02 lower would have been enough.
  • Only ship wheels whose startup profile covers long-context steps.
  • Watch cap minus reserved, not only cap minus peak. On the replica, reserved memory crept to 0.03 GiB below the cap before the OOM, while spare room over the peak still read 0.40 GiB. Treat the first allocator retry, and cap minus reserved under ~0.15 GiB, as alarms.
  • Make a death cheap: a restart supervisor, plus a solver that re-queues attempts for free on 500s and timeouts. A restart costs minutes, not the run.
