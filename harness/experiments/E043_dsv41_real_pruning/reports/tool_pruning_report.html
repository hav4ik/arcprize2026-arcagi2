Verdict.
  • Tool E128 2-bit is the deliverable. 82.5% pass@2 against 84.9% for the full model through the API, inside the 2-3 pp noise band of a single 2-attempt run, with a third of the routed experts at 2 bits. Same attempts: 28 won only by E128, 35 only by the full model (sign test p = 0.45).
  • Its cost is length: +28% output tokens and +3.1 turns per attempt, mostly from Python typos (14.2% of tool calls error, against 2.3%). On 4xL4 it runs 32.6 attempts/h (10.3 h per 334-attempt eval).
  • Tool E96 2/3-bit is not there yet: -9.0 pp pass@2. 37.4% of its tool calls error, and 58 attempts hit the 40-turn cap. It would be ~1.6x faster on 4xL4, so its 64-turn rerun (interrupted, see below) decides whether it is worth it.
  • The errors are character-level identifier typos from expert pruning, not quantization; truncated sampling does not remove them. More experts, fewer typos.
Status (2026-10-24). The Vast account's credit ran out at 14:46 UTC and the 8x RTX PRO 6000 box was stopped mid-run. 2. Where the accuracy goes

Pairs grouped by how often the full model solved them in its own 2 attempts. E128's gap is almost all in the borderline pairs, where the full model itself is a coin flip; it keeps 102 of the 105 easy pairs. E96 also drops 13 easy pairs (attempt accuracy 77% vs 91% for E128): pairs whose idea is easy but whose execution fails, the signature of typos and the turn cap. Both pruned models solve about a third of the pairs the full model missed twice. Bucketing by the reference's own results favours the reference (regression to the mean): a second full-model run would also lose some borderline pairs and win some hard ones.

The undefined names and missing modules are one-character slips (coms for comps, scpy for scipy): the pruned models lose some exact identifier copying. 5. 4xL4 serving (E042, dsv4-infer)

Kaggle-like 4xL4 (22.0 GiB usable per GPU, PCIe, no P2P), vLLM DeepSeek-V4.1 SM89 fork with Humming 2/3-bit expert kernels, TP4, util 0.93, prefix caching on. Weights are dummy tensors in the exact schemas; throughput is a multi-turn replay of real tool traffic, so prefix-cache reuse is realistic. The Engram table lives in pinned host RAM, not VRAM. The KV cache is tiny per token (compressed attention, ~1.5 KB per token per GPU).

A KV peak of ~0.8 is the practical edge: past it the prefix cache thrashes (uncached prefill triples at E96 x 96) before anything is preempted. E128 therefore runs at 40 streams and E96 at 80 or fewer. Throughput is close to the no-tool rate (E128 no-tool: 33.8 attempts/h) because 96-97% of each turn's prompt is a cached prefix.

7. How the models were built

  1. Expert statistics from 10M tokens of training-task tool rollouts (E031/E036/E038, DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017, s20x60 + header), replayed through the full model on the 8x RTX PRO 6000 box with an observer on every MoE layer: selections, router weights and per-expert output amplitudes.
  2. Keep sets by contribution on generated tokens, with tool-call-block tokens weighted x10. Without the weight the DSML tool-call format breaks first (E72: 0/10 valid first calls); x10 recovers most tool-block quality at less general-NLL cost than x30. The router (gate weight, bias, bias_vl) is sliced to the kept rows; top-6 routing stays.
  3. Engram: a 64 GiB top-frequency table (35.4% / 32.3% of the rows on layers 1 / 14) from a 252M-token census of the training rollouts. A weaker 2-bit model does not lean on Engram more:
Engram on tool E128 2-bitdev NLLtool-block NLL

  1. Low-bit experts: per-expert GPTQ with Humming's packing. Gate/up uint2 group 128; down uint2 (E128) or uint3 (E96) group 64; asymmetric, bf16 scales, damping 1.0 (experts see only ~3.4K calibration rows). Calibration: 1.4M tokens of tool rollouts replayed under each model's own mask, 10% of rows. Relative output error over the 40 layers: gate/up 2.5% (RTN 10.9%), down 5.5% (RTN 12.7%) for E128. 2-bit RTN is not usable.
  2. Export as a GPTQ-only checkpoint: backbone without routed experts, compact Engram files, gptq/layer_LL.safetensors. Parity on 64 dev traces: 0.79640 (export) vs 0.79652 (runtime masks). The HF copy was re-verified against the original checkpoint by byte ranges: router rows bit-exact at the kept ids, sampled tensors and Engram rows byte-equal, and every sampled dequantized expert matches its own original expert (cosine 0.89-0.93) and not its neighbour (~0).
