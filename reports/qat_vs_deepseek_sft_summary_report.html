Summary Report: Quantization-Aware Training (QAT) vs. Our Pruned DeepSeek-V4.1 (Tool E128) SFT Pipeline & Improvement Roadmap

Date: 2026-10-29  |  Target Architecture: Pruned DeepSeek-V4.1 (Tool E128, dsv41_e128_2bit) on 8× NVIDIA B200 GPUs

Executive Takeaway: Standard Quantization-Aware Training (QAT) simulates low-bit weight and scale discretization inside the forward() pass using a Straight-Through Estimator (STE) and updates FP32 master weights—often guided by Forward KL Distillation (QAD) against a frozen teacher. By contrast, our current Pruned DeepSeek-V4.1 SFT pipeline in train_dsv41_sft.py and dsv41_sft_model.py is a "De-quantize to BF16 → Train in Continuous BF16 → Data-Free Post-Training Re-Quantize" pipeline. Because 2-bit routed experts only have 4 discrete levels ({0, 1, 2, 3}) and shared_experts use UE8M0 power-of-two block scales, re-quantizing after continuous BF16 training introduces severe discretization shock and disrupts Havu's GPTQ Hessian calibration. Adopting QAT/QAD principles directly resolves these bottlenecks.

1. What is Quantization-Aware Training (QAT) & Distillation (QAD)?

While Post-Training Quantization (PTQ) compresses a model only after high-precision training is finished, QAT makes the training loop explicitly aware of the target low-bit quantization grid at every optimization step:

  1. Fake-Quantization in forward() + Straight-Through Estimator (STE) in backward(): Before every linear projection, master weights $W_{\text{master}}$ are quantized and immediately de-quantized back to the compute dtype: $$\widehat{W} = W_{\text{master}} + \text{stop\_grad}\big(\text{Dequant}(\text{Quant}(W_{\text{master}})) - W_{\text{master}}\big)$$ In the forward pass, activations see the exact discrete weights $\widehat{W}$ (including rounding errors, clamping, and block-scale jumps). In the backward pass, STE passes gradients $\nabla_{\widehat{W}} \mathcal{L}$ straight through to $W_{\text{master}}$.
  2. FP32 Master Weights: At SFT learning rates (1e-6 to 1e-5), single-step updates ($\Delta w \sim 10^{-6}$) are smaller than the BF16 unit in the last place ($\text{ULP} \approx 7.8 \times 10^{-4}$ for $|w| \approx 0.1$). QAT stores trainable master weights in FP32 so tiny updates accumulate smoothly across steps until they cross a discrete 2-bit or FP8 threshold.
  3. Preserving Calibrated Codebooks / Group Scales: When starting from an activation-calibrated checkpoint (such as GPTQ), the integer codes $q$ and zero-points $z$ were solved using the input activation Hessian ($H = X X^\top$) so quantization errors cancel across channels. Ultra-low-bit QAT preserves this calibration either by freezing $(q, z)$ and training the continuous group scales $s$, or by running STE with locked zero-points $z_{\text{orig}}$.
  4. Selective Precision Guardrails (NVIDIA Nemotron NVFP4, arXiv:2509.25149): Sensitive layers—embeddings, LM head, RMSNorms, attention projections, MoE router gates (DifferentiableGate), and the final ~15% of blocks—are kept in higher precision while heavy MoE expert FFNs operate in low precision.
  5. Quantization-Aware Distillation (QAD, arXiv:2601.20088): Training 2-bit/4-bit models with hard one-hot cross-entropy causes high gradient variance and shifts hidden-state norms entering frozen MoE routers. QAD minimizes Forward KL Divergence ($D_{\text{KL}}(P_{\text{teacher}} \parallel P_{\text{quant-student}})$ at $T=1.0$) against the teacher's full probability distribution, providing smooth gradients that stabilize MoE routing and multi-step reasoning.

2. Differences Between Our Current Pruned DeepSeek-V4.1 SFT Pipeline and QAT

3. Concrete Roadmap: How Our Pruned DeepSeek SFT Run Can Be Improved

Priority 0 1. Direct Group-Scale QAT (Trainable Per-Group Scales s with Frozen GPTQ q, z)

Instead of de-quantizing the 2-bit routed experts into dense BF16, adding a BF16 LoRA adapter, and re-running a destructive data-free L2 grid search at export:

  • Keep Havu's GPTQ 2-bit integer codes q and zero-points z frozen, and make the per-group scale tensor s ([N, K // group], where group=128 for w1, w3 and group=64 for w2) directly trainable in FP32 (parameterized as $s = s_{\text{orig}} \cdot (1 + \Delta s)$).
  • Why this is transformative:
    1. 100% Preservation of GPTQ Hessian Calibration: Because $q$ and $z$ never change, the intra-group error cancellation calibrated by Havu's GPTQ Hessian is completely preserved.
    2. Zero Export Discrepancy: At milestone export, we write s.to(torch.bfloat16) directly into gptq/layer_XX.safetensors alongside the original q and z. The exported checkpoint is bit-for-bit identical to the training forward pass.
    3. High Expressivity + Faster Export: Across 40 layers × 128 experts, group scales s provide 1.34B trainable parameters globally (167.8M per GPU)—exceeding r=32 LoRA (1.21B globally)—while eliminating the slow 16-point grid search at export.

Priority 0 2. Upgrade Trainable Master Weights (shared_experts & lora_A/B) to FP32

In dsv41_sft_model.py (LoRALinear.__init__ and SwiGLUExpert.__init__), store trainable master parameters in torch.float32 and cast to bfloat16 inside forward(). Across 40 layers, upgrading 1.15B trainable parameters per GPU from BF16 to FP32 costs only +2.3 GiB VRAM per GPU (on 180 GiB B200 GPUs with >45 GiB free headroom) and completely eliminates low-LR BF16 mantissa underflow.

Priority 1 3. Inject STE Fake-Quantization into SwiGLUExpert.forward & LoRALinear.forward

  • Shared Experts (FP8 + UE8M0 STE): Wrap shared_experts weights in w_ste = w + (dequant_fp8(quant_fp8(w)) - w).detach() during forward() so the optimizer immediately feels and compensates for any UE8M0 power-of-two scale doubling during training.
  • Routed Experts (Locked-z 2-Bit STE): If using LoRA on routed experts, retain Havu's original unpacked zero-point z_orig in memory at load time and apply fast 1-step group-wise STE rounding around z_orig in LoRALinear.forward and at export, rather than running the unweighted 16-point search over $z \in \{0,1,2,3\}$.

Priority 1 4. Add Quantized Validation Loss (val_quant_loss) for Milestone Selection

In train_dsv41_sft.py (evaluate_dsv41_validation_loss), compute and log val_quant_loss and val_quant_wic_alnum_loss with fake-quantization enabled. Select the best milestone checkpoint for Candidate B32 ARC-AGI-2 evaluation based on quantized validation loss rather than unquantized BF16 loss.

Priority 2 5. Enable Compact Engram Tables (Layers 1 & 14) & Forward KL Anchor (QAD)

  • Load the sharded engram/layer_01 and engram/layer_14 compact tables during SFT and pass engram_hash_ids into Dsv41SftModel.forward_hidden() so downstream experts train on the exact post-Engram residual stream used at inference.
  • Add a Forward KL regularization term ($0.2 \cdot D_{\text{KL}}(P_{\text{base}} \parallel P_{\text{quant}}) + 0.8 \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}}$) using the frozen base weights $W_0$ to prevent hidden-state drift across the 40 frozen 128-expert router gates.
